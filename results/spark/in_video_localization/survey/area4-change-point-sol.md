### Interpretation and evaluation

- **Score versus alarm:** CUSUM and kernel methods expose a statistic at each window; thresholding produces alarms. BOCPD exposes a posterior, but needs a defined scoring and alarm rule. A change score can peak at onset and then fall during an ongoing event. Therefore, evaluate both onset detection and anomaly-window ranking.
- **False-alarm control:** Calibrate the *complete detector* on normal streams. Report **ARL₀** (mean windows until a false alarm), **false alarms per hour**, and **detection delay** from event onset. Also report detection probability within a fixed delay and the delay–false-alarm trade-off. A BOCPD hazard is a prior on segment length, **not** an ARL₀ guarantee ([Adams and MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)).
- **Your sampling rate:** One decision every 2 seconds gives 1,800 decisions per hour. An ARL₀ target of 1,800 windows corresponds to one false alarm per hour **only under the specified continuous null and reset policy**. Calibrate with whole normal videos or continuous camera streams, preserving temporal dependence.
- **Failure modes:** A pre-change prefix must actually be normal. Scene changes and camera motion can resemble event onsets. Normal activity can drift, while updating the reference during an event can erase its evidence. An event lasting about three windows may end before a long comparison window or accumulated statistic responds.
- **Evidence gap:** The cited CPD results do **not** establish an improvement in UCF-Crime within-video AUC or camera-level false alarms per hour. REWARD’s frame AUC is not either metric.

### Try first

1. **One-sided CUSUM on \(p(\mathrm{yes})\):** cheapest causal baseline. Fit its normal reference on an early prefix; retain the raw score alongside the CUSUM statistic.
2. **Scan B-statistic on stored features:** tests distribution changes without a post-change model. Compare feature normalization and short block lengths against camera-motion false alarms.
3. **Online kernel CUSUM on stored features:** tests whether accumulated multivariate evidence improves alarm reliability. Compare its onset delay and within-video AUC separately; do not assume one improves the other.
